# Colour vision

A colour-vision mode changes the colours a colour-blind player uses to tell the sides, the chat roles and the player states apart. Off draws every one of them as it does today.

The war map's links use the mode's Arm, Core and contested colours, matched to the palette in use. Any other link colour is unchanged. The Arm and Core planet auras keep a run of shades. In protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia each shade moves to the shade of that side's colour nearest in lightness, and one shade is the mode's colour. In achromatopsia Arm's aura keeps its shades and Core's is drawn lighter. The independent aura, the planets, the rings and the star field stay as they are. Turning the mode off puts the aura pictures back as they were loaded.

The planet summary's two side icons keep their grey parts. Only each icon's coloured body takes that side's colour.

Chat status lines, news lines, Arm names and Core names take the mode's four role colours. A name whispers are locked onto uses the status colour. A muzzled name uses the mode's bad-health colour, and off keeps the dim tint. A latency picture keeps the shading of each grade. Green moves to the mode's good colour, orange to the middle colour and red to the bad colour. Its grey outline stays.

The mode in force when the main frame opens tints the chat. New chat lines, names and the planet summary take a new mode's colours on the next frame. Lines already in the chat log keep the colour they were printed in. The setting itself lives with the rest of the colour-vision preferences.

Shape cues add marks that stay when colour does not. While they are in effect, a planet a side holds carries that side's filled mark, a triangle for Arm and a square for Core, set just outside the planet at every magnification. A capitol also carries the mark of the side that founded it, drawn as the outline alone, on the other side of the planet. A capitol nobody holds carries that outline alone, so a filled mark always means a side holds the planet. In achromatopsia, Arm's links are dashed; every other link stays a solid line. The planet summary writes the side's name in front of each win count. The logo hides, and the line ends at the panel's inner edge. "Núcleo 117" is 43 pixels in the small green face, so the words start one pixel to the right of the battles count, which steps one pixel left while the cues are on. A chat line is printed with a bracketed role tag in front when its font is that role's. The tag is the Boneyards catalogue's, such as [SYS], [NEWS], [ARM] or [CORE], or that language's own bracketed side name. A latency picture is one, two or three bars. A name whispers are locked onto carries a lock, and a muzzled name a cross, and the name moves right to leave room. Off draws none of these. A line already in the log keeps the words it was printed with.
